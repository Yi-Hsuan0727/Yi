02 · Branding

Choosing a logo that works small

The mark had to survive a favicon, not just a presentation slide.

Two directions, designed from scratch.

  • The old serif wordmark was tiny in the navigation, and the AI logo references on my mood board were too detailed to survive that size.
  • On July 23 I presented two designs: a double-A with angular construction lines, and a circular badge following the founder's direction that looked like a car brand.
  • The double-A won: cleaner small, better in print, easier to extend.

Color & typography

Two brand colors and one single-weight typeface.

  • Color: Primary Blue (#0028FF) and Pure Black (#000000), each with a nine-step scale plus semantic and surface colors.
  • Type: Questrial has one weight, so hierarchy comes from size, tracking, and muted secondary text.

04 · Decisions & Priorities

What we chose, and what we cut

Five decisions, then a MoSCoW pass.

  • A survey, not a plain email box. The business team wanted email only. I argued that means re-asking everything on a call. The founder approved the survey in the first demo.
  • A globe, not a map. No data to filter, and a map needs upkeep.
  • WordPress, not Framer. Framer charged monthly for a custom domain. WordPress fits CRM and two languages better.
  • One action per page. A Contact button in the navigation, floating on mobile, and no direct email address.
  • Privacy and CRM. I flagged a privacy policy for every form. HubSpot's free plan handles contacts and auto-replies.

Several "must haves" were cut: the map became a globe, partner logos became a call to action, and the AI assistant and client login stayed out of scope.

September 21

The blank-site problem

The installed theme showed only a header and footer. An audit showed the code was complete: no page template was assigned and the meta boxes were empty. Claude Code wrote two scripts to fill them from the prototype's copy. I kept it off my WordPress account and worked only with files I uploaded.

Testing the live site against the prototype caught the rest, such as a carousel that never advanced and a language switcher wired up only once.
