TL;DR

Arizona Real Water is a learning platform for water education in Arizona. It serves real estate agents and brokers, educators, and the public, and comes from ASU's Arizona Water Innovation Initiative with the Arizona Department of Real Estate. The platform is live at realwateraz.org.

My role

01 · Context & Users

Water education for people who make real decisions about water

The platform delivers accessible water-education courses for Arizona communities, real estate professionals, and the public.

Agents and brokers

Real estate professionals who need to explain water and disclosure rules to their clients.

Educators

People who teach water topics and want course material and tools they can reuse. Educator courses are tagged separately from agent and broker courses.

The public

Anyone curious about water in Arizona can read the public content without an account.

How a learner moves through the platform

The flow was mapped in FigJam. A visitor first chooses a role, either educator or agent/broker. Onboarding then asks role-specific questions and leads to a short tutorial and a dashboard. From the dashboard a learner resumes in-progress courses, browses new ones, or opens saved courses. Finishing a course ends on a completion page and a badge for each module. The profile collects badges and certificates, and a chatbot stays available on the page.

02 · Scope & Priorities

What the platform must do first

A MoSCoW board sorted features into must have, should have, could have, and won't have, so the build could start with the essentials.

Must have

Onboarding that identifies the type of user and builds a learning plan, video progress and transcripts, learning objectives, badges, and varied quiz formats.

Should have

Optional profiles that save progress, an FAQ section, and an AI assistant for water questions.

Could have, won't have

Adaptive learning and regional tagging could follow. Full gamification and moderated discussion forums are out of scope.

03 · Design

Inside the ASU Design System, extended where needed

This is an ASU project, so the design starts from the ASU Design System and adds new patterns only when the project needs them.

Figma variables

I build the design tokens in Figma as variables, on top of the ASU Design System. The component list documents each component's states.

New patterns, when needed

When the project needs a pattern the design system doesn't have, I extend the system instead of starting a separate style.

Accessible by default

Transcripts and adjustable video speed were must-have features, and I fixed ARIA issues directly in the templates.

Key screens

The designs cover the learner's whole path. These are the Figma designs, and some still use placeholder text and images.

Lessons and quizzes

A lesson page pairs the video with a View Transcript button and an overview. Quizzes support five question types: scale, single choice, short answer, long answer, and checkbox. Each answer can show a correct or an error message.

Resources and profile

Component library

I organized the components in one Figma list: buttons in four colors and three sizes, each with default, hover, disabled, focus, and icon states, plus cards, certificate, badge, download, flip card, and carousel components. Notes beside each one record rules such as the 8 px radius.

Edge cases

Error pages get their own designs, so a wrong link or a blocked page still explains what happened and offers a way back.

05 · Privacy & Data

Writing down what we collect before saying it out loud

A learning platform with accounts, forms, video, and an AI assistant collects personal data. I helped document it in an inventory that the privacy page draws on.

Inventory

Every data point, one row each

The inventory covers analytics, forms, video embeds, and the AI assistant. Gaps are marked "fill in" rather than guessed, since a reviewer would rather see a gap than a retention period nobody set.

Decision

Link to ASU's statement

The privacy page links to the ASU Privacy Statement as the governing document and acts as a site-specific supplement, instead of copying it.

Decision

A default retention period

Four years by default: two license renewal cycles of two years each, plus a window for research analysis. Rows that differ explain why.

Decision

Contact form, two paths

The form stores submissions in the Drupal database and also sends a notification email. Both paths are disclosed, and the database copy needs a purge schedule.

The AI assistant

The Real Waterbot answers questions about Arizona water. My part was the interface and the privacy documentation, not the model. I helped prepare a question list for the people who built the bot, grouped into vendor and contract, data flow, logging, and what the interface tells users. The answers decide how much of the privacy page's AI section is needed.

Not legal advice. Entity, student-record scope, and consent mechanics are calls for university counsel and governance. The inventory prepares those conversations.
